<p>In fifth grade, John-Paul Dombrowski got very depressed, and stayed that way through high school. He remembers sitting across from a therapist thinking two things at once: <em>it sucks that I feel this bad</em>, and <em>you are really bad at your job</em>.</p><p>He is a therapist now, in Steubenville, running a practice called Koum Counseling &amp; Wellness and training the therapists in it.</p><p>Ryan sat down with him at Franciscan, where the two of them overlapped as students, for a conversation that goes somewhere most conversations about mental health do not. Not whether therapy works. Not whether Christians should go. The question underneath both: what is actually happening when one person helps another, and what is the helper bringing into the room without meaning to.</p><p>The old idea JP builds on is the wounded healer. Your wounds are what let you recognize someone else's. They are also what make you work out your own problems through the person sitting in front of you. Both are true at once, which is why he says the real question is never <em>am I wounded</em> but <em>can I see it, and can I step back from it</em>.</p><p>From there it opens out. Why "I'm a good helper if my people get better" is a belief that guarantees burnout. Why Aristotle would say friendship is good in itself and health is only a by-product, and what goes wrong when we forget the difference. Why the phrase <em>my back pain</em> is doing more damage than the back pain. Why the prodigal son is a story about shame rather than sin. And a 5am routine that is thirty minutes of movement, thirty of silence, and forty of telling God the truth, which usually starts with <em>I don't want to be here right now</em>.</p><p>The last ten minutes are the ones to stay for. JP makes a case that the most spiritually corrosive sentence in ordinary life is a completely normal one: <em>when this happens, then I'll be okay.</em> He thinks it is the same move, whether the blank is weight, money, a diagnosis, or a season of prayer. And he thinks it is what Christ was talking about when he said the kingdom of heaven is already here.</p><p><strong>IN THIS EPISODE</strong></p><ul><li>(03:58) The therapist who did not help, and the fifth grader who knew it</li><li>(05:08) Finding psychology and thinking: this is the language I have been looking for</li><li>(07:39) The savior complex, in therapists and chiropractors alike.
